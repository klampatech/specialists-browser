The Specialists buy menu redesign

A redesign of the in-game buy menu for the Half-Life 1 mod The Specialists. This page holds the mockups and everything an implementing agent needs to know about the game and the intended behavior. The mockups are static. The commands that drive the menu are being mapped separately by the project owner.

Updated 2026-11-02, after the playtest. The frames below follow what the playtest changed: the browse screen’s rail is the weapon categories (six of them, and the akimbo weapons are not among them — a player who wants the akimbo Berettas is looking at the Berettas, so the akimbo version is offered on the gun, on its attachments screen), the HUD slot 5 card says Special Purpose (the game’s own name for that slot: the akimbo versions and the Golden Colts), the cards no longer carry a “+ Add” button (the card’s own number opens its list), and the panels no longer explain themselves in their footers. The akimbo version is the gun bought twice — the game’s own rule, and the server’s spawn gives the player the gun and the pair: the gun in its own card, the pair in the card 5. So the akimbo Five-seveN costs $2,500, not $1,250, and the loadout shows both: the Five-seveN in the pistols card, the Akimbo Five-seveN in Special Purpose. The key table is the port’s key space: the rail’s keys and the HUD slots are two numberings, and the loadout screen has the accessory on 6, Random on 7 and Clear on 8. The panels no longer explain themselves: the three lines of footer text are gone, and nothing is highlighted by default when a category opens — a list that opens with a row lit reads as “this one is chosen”, and picking a weapon takes the player to another screen anyway.

1. Game context

Confirmed by the project owner:

  • The game is a Half-Life 1 mod. Weapons live in numbered slots, as in vanilla Half-Life, and the player has 5 slots.
  • Slots are fixed by weapon type. Slot 1 is melee, grenades, swords and unarmed. Slot 2 is pistols. Slot 3 is SMGs. Slot 4 is rifles and shotguns. Slot 5 is the special-purpose slot: the akimbo versions of the pistols and SMGs, and the Golden Colts. (The owner was fairly but not fully sure about slot 5; the game’s own weapon table has since answered it.)
  • A slot can hold more than one weapon as long as the budget allows.
  • The menu is number-driven (0–9, plus “A” for a tenth row). Clicking a row also works, and B closes the panel.
  • The budget is a fixed amount ($30,000 in the examples) spent on a loadout. It is not a per-round economy.
  • Attachments (Silencer, Lasersight, Flashlight) are bought for a gun. In the current game the attachments are bought before the gun.

Unverified, treat as assumptions: the M60E3 goes in slot 5 (the game’s own weapon kinds put it in the rifles, so the mockups follow the game). The meaning of the header’s “Slots” value (it read 61 in the current UI) is unknown, so the mockups show “slots in use out of 5” instead.

2. Problems with the current menu

  • Previous menu levels stay visible, dimmed, beneath the current one. Numbers repeat down the list (several rows labelled 1, 2, 3…), which is confusing.
  • Buying a gun is a multi-step trip (category, weapon, Options, Buy), and then the player must pick “Use New Config” to actually use it. Players often skip that and don’t fight with what they bought.
  • Attachments must be purchased before the gun, which is backwards from how players think.
  • Categories (Handguns, SMGs, Rifles, Shotguns, Special Purpose, Player Accessory) don’t match the in-game slots. Knives sit under Special Purpose but belong in slot 1.
  • Config management (Empty, Reset, Save, Load, Use New) is exposed as separate menu actions.

3. Design principles

  1. The current loadout is always live. Whatever the player has bought is what they spawn with. There is no “use config” step.
  2. One level on screen at a time. No stacked or repeated menus. Always show where the player is (breadcrumb) and the budget.
  3. Organize by slot, not category. Number keys match the in-game slot numbers.
  4. Stage, then commit. Attachments are chosen with the gun and committed in one action. The command order (attachments first, gun second) is hidden from the player.
  5. Loadouts are tabs, saved automatically. There is no Save or Load step.
  6. Never hide an option, explain it. Unaffordable items stay visible and say why.

4. Screens and flow

  1. The player presses B. The panel opens on Loadout (screen 1) showing the active loadout.
  2. They press a slot number (or click “+ Add”). The Browse screen (2) opens for that slot. The rail lets them jump to another slot.
  3. They pick a weapon. The Attachments screen (3) opens. They toggle attachments, then press Enter to add the gun plus its attachments, which returns them to the Browse screen or Loadout (implementer’s choice, but returning to the same slot’s list suits buying a second pistol).
  4. They press Enter on the Loadout screen, or close with B. The current loadout is used on the next spawn. Closing never discards anything.

Number keys on the Browse screen select weapons, and on the Loadout screen they open slots. Because the meaning changes per screen, the screen title and breadcrumb must always be visible. The rail’s keys and the HUD slots are two numberings: the rail’s 4 is the rifles alone, while the HUD slot 4 holds the rifles and the shotguns. These bindings are proposals and can be remapped to whatever the command layer supports.

7. Behavior rules

  • Implicit loadout: purchases go straight into the active loadout (A, B or C). Switching tabs switches which loadout is live. Edits save automatically.
  • Multiple weapons per slot: allowed while the budget lasts. Buying a second pistol adds it and does not replace the first.
  • Adding a gun with attachments: compute the total (gun plus selected attachments). If it exceeds the money left, disable “Add” and show the missing amount. On success, issue the attachment purchases first and the gun second, then update the budget.
  • Removing: selecting an owned weapon removes it and refunds its price. Removing a gun also removes its attachments (confirm whether the game refunds them).
  • Clear: empties the active loadout and refunds everything. Random: fills the active loadout within budget.
  • Budget header: money left, a bar for the share spent, and slots in use out of 5.
  • The akimbo version: it is the gun bought twice — the game’s own rule, and the server turns the second copy into the pair. So the row on the gun’s attachments screen costs the gun’s price again (the akimbo Five-seveN is $2,500, not $1,250), and the loadout shows both: the gun in its own card, the pair in the card 5. The two cards’ prices add up to what the player paid.

10. Open questions

  • What does the header’s “Slots” number (61 in the current UI) actually count? The mockups show slots in use out of 5.
  • Is M60E3 definitely slot 5, and what else is in slot 5?
  • Do any limits exist beyond money (per-slot or weapon-count caps, map or mode restrictions)? If so, add a “not allowed” state distinct from “can’t afford”.
  • Does removing a gun refund its attachments?
  • Where should the player land after adding a gun: the same slot’s list or the Loadout screen?
  • Are keyboard arrow keys supported by the menu system, or only number keys and the mouse?

11. Scope

Not designed: the SMG, rifle, shotgun and accessory lists (they reuse the Browse layout), the Random and Clear confirmations, a first-time hint for new players, and any in-world HUD changes (the weapon bar at the top of the screen is untouched).
